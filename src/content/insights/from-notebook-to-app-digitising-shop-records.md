Many good businesses run on a notebook. The owner knows the customers, the prices and roughly what is on the shelves. The trouble starts when the business grows: more products, more staff, more customers buying on credit. A notebook cannot easily tell you what you sold last month, which items are running low or whether the cash in the drawer matches the day's sales.

A sales and stock app can answer those questions in seconds. The move goes most smoothly when a few things are sorted out first.

## Decide what you most need to know

Before choosing any app, write down the three questions you most want answered. For example:

- How much did I sell today, and does the cash match?
- Which items are about to run out?
- How much does each customer owe me?

These questions tell you which features matter. An app that answers them well is more useful than one with many features you will not use.

## Clean up your product list

Your product list is the foundation. Before you start, write out every item you sell with:

- A clear name, so two similar items cannot be confused
- The selling price
- What it cost you, if you want to see profit
- How many you have in stock today

This is often the longest step. It is also worth doing even if you never use an app, because many owners discover items they had forgotten about or were selling below cost.

If you have many products, prepare the list in a spreadsheet. Many apps can import it in one go instead of you typing each item.

## Count your stock on a quiet day

The app's stock figures are only as good as the starting count. Choose a quiet day, count everything and enter those numbers as your opening stock. From then on, the app reduces stock as items are sold.

## Agree how staff will use it

If other people sell in your shop, agree the rules before the first day:

- Every sale goes through the app, with no exceptions for small sales
- Who is allowed to change prices
- How mistakes are corrected, and who approves the correction

Give each staff member their own login or PIN where the app allows it, so you can see who made each sale.

## Think about the internet and your phone

If your connection is unreliable, choose an app that keeps working offline and does not lose sales when the data runs out. Check that it runs well on the phone or tablet you already have.

## Plan for backups

Your records will soon be valuable. Find out how the app backs up your data and how you would restore it if the phone were lost, stolen or broken. Then actually make a backup.

## Run both for a short time

For the first week or two, some owners keep the notebook alongside the app. It builds confidence that nothing is being missed and helps staff get used to the new routine. Once the app's totals match what you expect, the notebook can go.
